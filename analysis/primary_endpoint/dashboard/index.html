Reading the change

  • No control visit. The change also holds whatever else happens between the two recordings: the time between them, the time of day, and the first recording's cuff occlusion.
  • Few pairs. Most participants miss a value before or after. Whether they would have changed alike is not known.
  • The capillary may differ. Each recording's loops were chosen blind and on their own, so before and after may come from different capillaries. Aim 1 shows how much that alone moves a value.
  • Regression to the mean. A value that is high by chance tends to come out lower the next time, which looks like a fall.
  • Several tests. Each study and the pooled analysis are tested, without a correction for multiple tests.
  • Pooled. It takes the two studies' participants as different people.

How the numbers were made

The protocol (PROTOCOL.md in the repository) was fixed with the PI before the first line was placed: velocity round the whole capillary loop in the resting flow, signal-to-noise as the quality control, and one reference capillary per recording with ranked backups for when it fails. The observer placed every line blind: the recordings were listed under codes, results were hidden, and the measured range was fixed to the official rest. Nothing here was looked at until every recording had its lines. Two changes to the analysis came after, below.

Aim 1 compares each participant's two before-exposure recordings, one per visit. Recordings after exposure are used by aim 2 only; they appear here in the counts of what the quality control keeps.

The protocol's settings

SettingValueNote

Fixed before unblinding

  • — lines on — recordings had been saved without the whole-loop role: returning to a recording seen before restored the role of a try-out line, and the next lines kept it. They were re-saved as whole loops from their own points and space-time diagrams; the blocks and the pooled pattern shift came out exactly as saved. The app now fixes the role during blind placement.
  • A recording skipped by mistake was placed, still blind, before anything was unblinded.
  • Lines whose limbs cross (a twisted loop) are not candidates, a rule clarified during placement: the next loop in the ranking takes their place.
  • — try-out lines from before blind placement, on two recordings, are not whole loops and are not used.
  • One recording has no line at all: its picture moved further than the frame is high during the rest, so no line stays in it.

Changed after unblinding

  • Aim 1 is pooled over both studies, as the study progress report states. The protocol first said "for each study"; it was corrected after the per-study results had been seen, and they are still shown beside the pooled one.
  • Aim 2 looks only at the nicotine visit, as the student and the PI agreed verbally before unblinding. The protocol's first text took the participants with all four recordings and compared the change on the nicotine visit with the control visit's. That analysis had been computed and seen at unblinding; its tables are kept with the data.
  • The correction added participants with a complete nicotine visit but no complete control visit. Their recordings without lines were placed blind, under codes and by the same rules, before aim 2 was computed again; none of their loops counted.
  • The other tabs describe the loops and recordings as they stood at unblinding, including the recordings after the control visit, which aim 2 no longer uses.

Where the data come from

analysis/primary_endpoint/report.py reads the saved data sets through capillary.aims, the protocol's analysis, and writes these tables; analysis/primary_endpoint/secondary.py adds aim 1 pooled and aim 2 from capillary.aims' tables. Run it again after any change to the lines, then ask Claude to replace the tables here. Recordings appear here only by study, participant and visit, so that a later blind placement of the same recordings stays blind.
